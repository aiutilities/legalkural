# Compressed Title

## Rent Is Business. Living Is Residence.

---

# Kural-Inspired Tamil Insight

> **வாடகை தொழிலெனினும் வாழ்விடம் வீடே;
> பயன்பாடு காண்பதே நீதியின் வழியே.**

### Transliteration

> **Vāḍagai tozhil eninum vāzhviḍam vīḍē;
> Payanpāḍu kāṇbadē nīthiyin vazhiyē.**

### Plain Meaning

Even when renting is a business,
the place where people live remains a home.

Justice must examine how the premises are actually used.

---

# Kural-Inspired English Insight

> **Profit may describe the owner.
> Purpose must describe the place.**

---

# Human Conflict

This dispute was never merely about tax.

It was about two different ways of seeing the same building.

The authorities viewed it as a business.

The occupants experienced it as a home.

The legal conflict therefore became:

- Commercial identity of the owner
- Residential use of the occupant

---

# Universal Principle

## Substance is greater than Label.

A legal classification should follow the activity actually carried out inside the premises.

Not merely the owner's business model.

---

# Judicial Principle

The Court's reasoning can be compressed into four questions.

1. Who actually uses the property?

2. What do they do there?

3. Which statutory definition applies?

4. Was procedural fairness followed?

Everything else follows from these four questions.

---

# Social Principle

Commercial tariffs imposed on shared hostels ultimately burden the residents.

Those residents often choose hostels because independent housing is unaffordable.

Equal residential activity deserves equal legal treatment.

---

# Natural Justice Principle

> **Before increasing a person's burden, the law must first hear that person.**

Changing a property from residential to commercial classification materially increases financial liability.

The Court found that the authorities failed to establish that prior notice had been given before the reclassification.

Accordingly, the demand notices suffered from a procedural defect independent of the classification issue itself.

---

# Compression Test

## The Case in One Sentence

A hostel does not become commercial merely because its owner earns income when the occupants use it as their residence.

## The Case in Two Words

**Usage Decides**

---

# Expansion

The authorities treated the hostel properties as commercial because the owners operated them as businesses.

The Court separated the owner's commercial activity from the occupants' residential use.

The residents used the premises for sleeping, bathing, eating and ordinary daily living.

Those activities were substantially similar to the activities performed by tenants living in rented residential apartments.

Therefore, the Court held that the functional use of the premises should determine the legal classification.

The Court further held that the respondents had failed to establish compliance with principles of natural justice before changing the tariff classification.

The commercial demand notices were therefore quashed.

The Court nevertheless clarified that the decision applies only where the factual use of the premises is genuinely residential.

# Reusable Rule

Whenever the legal nature of a property is disputed:

- Determine actual use.
- Ignore labels.
- Apply the statutory definition.
- Verify procedural fairness.
- Then decide.

---

# Editorial Boundary

This editorial interpretation is based on the judicial reasoning contained in the judgment.

It should not be understood as declaring that every hostel automatically qualifies for residential classification.

Each case depends upon its own facts.
